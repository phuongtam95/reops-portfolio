Tool stack · Self-serve

Moved researchers onto Rally: a third of studies went self-serve and tool spend fell 15%+

  • Block
  • Part of the setup team
  • Trainer and coach
  • Six-figure tool budget

The problemOps ran about 3 studies a week, up to 5 at peak, and every recruit and calendar invite went through us. We were the bottleneck, and that didn't scale.

What I did
  • I helped set up Rally and coached researchers to recruit and schedule their own participants once I'd pulled the list.
  • My call: Rally only lets a screener link out once, so I duplicated studies per user group. More setup for me, but we could recruit more for slow groups or field priority groups first.
  • After quarterly usage and spend reviews, I recommended consolidating around Rally, which cut tool spend at least 15%. I also piloted it for smaller surveys to save Qualtrics for the big ones.

How I counted: ~3 studies a week × ~50 weeks × one third ≈ 50 studies a year.

What I learnedSetting up the tool was the easy part. Getting people comfortable using it took longer, and that's where the coaching really mattered.

Privacy · High-stakes research

Ran privacy-safe survey ops for Block's published Modern Earner research

  • Block
  • 3–6 months
  • Ops for the Cash App sample
  • Vendor: FactWorks

The problemThe team needed survey answers linked to internal customer data, but the outside vendor couldn't know who our customers were. A slip would have meant a privacy incident on a public report.

What I did
  • I set it up so FactWorks only ever saw an anonymous ID, never a customer ID.
  • I handled distribution and incentives across 3–6 months of survey batches.
  • My call: I kept the safer two-redirect setup, knowing it meant cleanup later. People ended up with two or more rows each, so I merged them by hand.

What I learnedBouncing between tools kept the data safe, but it got messy. Next time I'd try one redirect: ask what we need up front, then let people finish on the vendor's side.

How it worked

Screener problems I kept seeing, and the fix for each

ProblemMany questions looked the same except for the time frame, so people misread them.

FixHighlight the time frame in every question.

In the last 6 months, how often did you send money to someone?

In the past year, how often did you send money to someone?

ProblemThe wrong people were getting through screeners.

FixAdd checks before anyone gets invited.

  1. Attention check
  2. Uses the product enough
  3. Answers match behavior
  4. Invited

FixPull a bigger list or relax the criteria before raising the incentive.

Research quality · AI with human review

Kept thrown-out survey responses to 2–5% instead of 10–15%, saving ~$500–1,000 per survey

  • Block
  • 1,500–2,000-response surveys
  • Advisor to researchers
  • Claude and Gemini, human-reviewed

The problemSurveys paid $3–5 per completed response, $3K–10K in incentives each. When lists or screeners were loose, 10–15% of those paid responses ended up thrown out.

What I did
  • When the same screener problem kept coming up, I fixed it once and made it the standard.
  • I targeted lists with SQL and checked what people said against what they actually did in the product, so bad fits got caught before we paid for them.
  • My call: I used Claude and Gemini to flag weak questions, but every change went through me first. They were good at spotting patterns, not at judging our customers.

What I learnedRelax the criteria before you raise the incentive. Bigger payouts mostly bring in people who are there for the money.
